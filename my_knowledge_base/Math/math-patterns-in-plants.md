# Math Patterns in Plants

Plants exhibit various mathematical principles, primarily related to geometry and number theory.

### 1. Fibonacci Sequences and Phyllotaxis
**Phyllotaxis** refers to the arrangement of leaves, seeds, or petals. Many plants exhibit spirals that follow the **Fibonacci sequence** (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13...).
*   **Examples:** Sunflowers (seed spirals), Pinecones, and the leaf arrangement on Aloe plants.
*   **Purpose:** This growth pattern ensures that each leaf or seed gets maximum exposure to sunlight and space.

### 2. Symmetry
*   **Radial Symmetry:** The plant can be divided into equal parts by any cut through the center (e.g., most flowers).
*   **Bilateral Symmetry:** The plant can be divided into two equal halves (e.g., many leaves).

### 3. Fractals
Fractals are patterns that repeat at different scales (self-similarity).
*   **Examples:** Tree branching and the structure of fern leaves. The smaller branches mimic the overall shape of the larger tree or leaf.

### 4. Geometric Growth and Packing
Nature uses math to solve packing problems—fitting the most items into a space. This is often tied to the **Golden Ratio ($\phi$)**, which allows for optimal packing of seeds and leaves.
